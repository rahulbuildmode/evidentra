1–2. Executive summary and recommendation

Recommendation: REVISE (do not stop; do not build the full product yet). Evidentra targets a real, documented regulatory environment: India licenses nearly all device classes on dossier-style submissions, a parliamentary committee reports repeated query rounds and opacity, and the government is drafting faster statutory timelines. But no source found measures how often CDSCO queries or delays are caused by missing or inconsistent documents, no source gives a verified count of Indian licensees or regulatory consultants, and willingness to pay, time saved and accuracy are all unproven. Revise the plan to: (a) lead with regulatory consultants / small RA firms (repeat submissions across clients) rather than single-product startups; (b) scope v1 to CDSCO Class B and C manufacturing licence dossiers; (c) run the cheap tests on page 3 before any engineering spend. The market model below is an assumption-driven range (base ≈ ₹63 Cr TAM, ₹19 Cr SAM, ₹1.1 Cr 3-yr SOM), not a forecast.

4. Indian regulatory landscape and evidence of customer pain

  • Legal base: Medical Devices Rules, 2017 (G.S.R. 78(E), 31 Jan 2017) under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act; four risk classes A–D P CDSCO MDR page. That page lists 34 notifications; consolidated text dated 15 Feb 2023, latest listed entry 2 Jun 2023 (G.S.R. 409(E)). Later changes (below) are known only from secondary reports — current consolidated text must be verified before product logic is built.
  • Scope: All devices fall under licensing except Class A non-sterile, non-measuring, which are exempt but need registration (G.S.R. 777(E), 14 Oct 2022 P CDSCO MDR page above; 31 Oct 2025 notice S morulaa.com). Class C/D licensing extended to previously non-notified devices from 1 Oct 2023 S ssrana.in.
  • Process change, Oct 2025: reported CDSCO directive (23 Oct 2025) requires central classification of Class A/B/IVD devices before State authorities grant licences; SUGAM classification module live 27 Nov 2025 (devices) S morulaa.com (above). Class A/B licences: State authority (MD-5) after notified-body audit; C/D: Central authority (MD-9) S altacit.com.
  • Pain evidence (qualitative): BCG/AiMeD report (May 2026) lists sequential approvals, high certification costs and "longer turnaround for Class C validation due to high dependence on CDSCO" as MSME constraints S BCG PDF. The 2025 committee reported manufacturers shifting to Vietnam/Malaysia (medicaldialogues.in). Not shown: that documentation errors, rather than inspection backlog or staffing, drive these delays.
  • Fees (context): reported govt fees Class A/B ₹5,000/site + ₹500/device; Class C/D ₹50,000/site + ₹1,000/device S agileregulatory.com (sources conflict; verify on CDSCO).

Method and source integrity

Sources were fetched 21 Oct 2026. Primary = CDSCO pages/FAQ read directly; secondary = news, law-firm, consultant and vendor pages (self-interested; unaudited). Not obtained: the committee report itself, Business Standard (HTTP 403), official licensee counts, any CDSCO query/pendency statistics, and the amended consolidated MDR text after Jun 2023. The CDSCO "Medical Device & Diagnostics" page is undated and appears partly out of date. No figure here is invented; unsourced numbers are tagged A. General regulatory delay is not treated as proof of documentation-caused delay.

11. Major risks and five assumptions to validate

Risks:
  • Cause of delays is mostly inspection/QMS audit, not dossier quality
  • Regulatory text drifts (draft 2026 timelines; Oct 2025 workflow change) → stale logic
  • Wrong-answer liability; users over-trust output
  • Consultants see tool as threat; CDSCO builds AI pre-screen itself
  • Small, price-sensitive market (₹19 Cr base SAM)
Assumptions:
  1. A1 Documentation gaps cause a material share of CDSCO queries
  2. A2 Consultants will pay for dossier QA
  3. A3 Tool finds real gaps/contradictions at useful precision
  4. A4 ≥₹15k/pack or ≥₹3 L/yr is acceptable
  5. A5 Firm/consultant counts support SAM

13. What can be claimed today vs unproven

Supportable now:
  • CDSCO requires structured, multi-part, online dossiers across risk classes (primary)
  • A parliamentary committee reported repeated queries and opacity; govt drafted shorter timelines (secondary)
  • Rival tools exist; few state India-specific support; Pure Global does
Unproven:
  • That document errors cause CDSCO queries/delays; any % rework
  • Time saved, accuracy, willingness to pay, consultant demand
  • Number of licensees/consultants; market size beyond assumption
  • That competitors lack a feature (only "not stated")

14. Final recommendation and key sources

REVISE: narrow to consultants + Class B/C dossiers; run A1–A3 within ~6 weeks; proceed to build only if A1 and A2 pass, stop if A1 <25% of query items or no paid pilots.
